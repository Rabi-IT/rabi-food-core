# Product Context

## Customer profiles

Two distinct profiles exist:

**Discoverer** — does not know the product yet. Needs education before committing. Lands on the landing/presentation page. Arrives via search, social media, or word of mouth.

**Decider** — already knows what they want. Needs minimum friction. Lands directly on the product listing (vitrine). Arrives via QR code at the establishment or a shared link from another customer.

## Why a landing page exists

The subscription model is fundamentally different from what the market is used to. Per lean startup principles, the project operates under extreme uncertainty. The landing page must communicate value *before* the customer enters the subscription flow:

- Convenience and organization
- More competitive pricing
- Fresh food
- Helps local businesses generate value for their region: reduces dependency on loans, avoids abusive interest rates, fair pricing model with no per-sale commission — unlike marketplaces that exploit local businesses

This last point is a core differentiator and needs further elaboration.

## Vitrine is not the place for explanation

The vitrine should lead to the landing page, not explain the product itself. The vitrine is where the customer selects the product they want to subscribe to. There is no "subscribe" button that leads to another listing — the vitrine *is* the listing.

## Subscription model

**Always a commitment, never a trial.**

The customer signs up and pays for the chosen plan. Deliveries happen as scheduled.

**Cancellation without refund:**
- The customer can cancel renewal at any time
- Already-paid deliveries continue until exhausted
- No penalty, no refund — the subscription simply stops renewing

**Cancellation with refund (Brazilian Consumer Protection Code — CDC):**
- Only within 7 days of the **first delivery** (not signup date)
- Refund is automatic and legally required
- Returns the value of undelivered orders

**Auto-renewal is not a toggle.** It is a fact of the subscription. The UI must clearly state this rather than giving the customer an option to disable it. Framing: *"your subscription renews automatically at the end of the cycle. You can cancel renewal at any time."*

## Substitution vs refund (important distinction)

These are two different customer intents and must be communicated separately:

- **Substitution** — "I don't want this product, I'd prefer another one" → no cost, no friction, handled as a change request
- **Refund** — "I don't want anything, I want my money back" → only valid within 7 days of first delivery

## Summary screen (final wizard step)

The summary is the most important moment in the flow — where trust is built and the customer decides whether to commit. It must answer questions the customer hasn't asked out loud:

- When will I receive? (concrete dates, not just weekdays)
- How much will I pay and when?
- What happens if I want to cancel? (substitution vs refund distinction)
- Can I change later?

Treat the summary as a trust-building screen, not a bureaucratic checklist.
